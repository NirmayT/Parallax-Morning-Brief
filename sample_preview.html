The Parallax
Expectation Versus Outsourcing Reality

Expectations that artificial intelligence would rapidly displace service workers contrast with ongoing labor resilience in primary global back-office hubs. Because business process outsourcing accounts for a significant share of employment in these regions, widespread automation should manifest there first. The lack of rising joblessness suggests that corporate technology adoption has not yet triggered broad workforce retrenchment.

Worth knowing

India and Philippines Unemployment Continues Downward Trend

Unemployment rates in the Philippines and India remain near 5% and 6% respectively, holding well below levels recorded in 2021. The ongoing labor strength occurs despite widespread expectations of technology-driven job loss in service sectors.

Torsten Slok Highlights Absence of AI Job Disruption

Apollo Chief Economist Torsten Slok pointed out that an expected white-collar labor shock has not materialized in major emerging workforce centers. Macroeconomic data indicates that job creation in key technical and back-office hubs remains stable.

Outsourcing Hubs Represent Primary Indicator for AI Displacement

Large-scale white-collar automation is expected to impact call centers, IT support, and back-office processing ahead of other corporate functions. Because India and the Philippines concentrate these business operations, their employment figures provide an early gauge for global labor displacement.

The open question

Why do macroeconomic analysts monitor labor market data to evaluate technological trends?

Labor market data reveals whether new business technologies are expanding economic output or directly replacing human workers. When companies adopt labor-saving software at scale, the initial impact typically appears as rising unemployment or slowing hiring in exposed job categories. Investors track aggregate employment figures to gauge whether structural technological changes are altering overall corporate productivity and spending.
